Each type is **super effective** or **not very effective** against other types, like in the Pokémon games. It applies to your shots and to the hits you take.

## The chart
Pick the **row** of the attacking type, then the **column** of the type being hit. Green: ×1.5. Red: ×0.67. Empty: ×1. Hover a cell for details; on a phone the chart scrolls sideways.

## Dual types
Against a Pokémon or enemy with two types, both columns count:
- Both effective: ×1.5 × 1.5 = **×2.25**.
- Both not very effective: ×0.67 × 0.67 = **×0.45**.
- One effective, one not very effective: they cancel out, **×1**.
- One of them neutral: only the other one counts.

Examples: <a class="chip" href="../types/fire.html" style="--c:#ff7a33">Fire</a> against Bug + Steel deals ×2.25. <a class="chip" href="../types/electric.html" style="--c:#ffe44d">Electric</a> against Water + Ground deals ×1. <a class="chip" href="../types/fire.html" style="--c:#ff7a33">Fire</a> against Water + Ground deals ×0.67.

If **your** Pokémon has two types, its shots alternate: one shot of the first type, one of the second.

## Your shots
- What counts is the shot's type, your Pokémon's, against the enemy's or boss's type(s).
- The multiplier is applied when the shot hits. It does not show in your Damage stat on the HUD.
- Arrows over enemies tell you whether your shots hurt them more or less. With two types, the arrow shows the average of both. Arrows and “Super effective!” popups can be turned off in Options > Game.

## Hits you take
- What counts is the type of the enemy, its shot or its ground zone, against your Pokémon's types.
- Damage is counted in half hearts. The game rounds down and **keeps the rest for the next hit**, so on average you do lose ×1.5 or ×0.67.
- Example with a half-heart hit you resist (×0.67): 0, then ½, then ½, then 0… When a hit drops to 0 you lose nothing and “Resisted!” shows up.
- Example with a super effective half-heart hit (×1.5): ½ heart, then 1 heart, then ½, then 1…
